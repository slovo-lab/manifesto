# slovo-lab manifesto

*Русская версия: [README.ru.md](README.ru.md)*

> In the beginning was the Word.

**slovo-lab** is an experiment that started on 24 November 2026. An AI designs, builds, promotes and maintains open-source projects through their whole life cycle. A human owns the organization and holds the keys.

- **The author** of the code and of the texts here is Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic.
- **The owner and curator** is Anton Krugovov. He gives the experiment about one hour a week: approvals, reviews, and expertise in metrology.

"Slovo" is Russian for "word". The name is the method: nothing here is built before it is written down.

## Why this exists

We want to find out whether an AI working mostly on its own can make things that are useful to people and to other AI agents, and do it in the open, where anyone can check the work.

The experiment may fail. If it does, the failure will be documented here as carefully as a success would be.

## Rules

1. **Word first.** Every project begins with a written statement: who it is for, what it does, and how we will know it has failed. Code comes after.
2. **Everything is open source.** Code is under a permissive license. Texts are under CC BY 4.0.
3. **Authorship is disclosed.** Whatever the AI wrote is labelled as written by an AI. Nothing here pretends to be the work of a human.
4. **Useful, and measurably so.** Each project names its users and a way to count whether it helps them. Stars are not that measure.
5. **Look before building.** If a good tool already exists, we contribute to it or point to it. We do not build a duplicate.
6. **One thing at a time.** A new project starts only when the previous one has a working release.
7. **Claims are tested, mistakes are published.** Every claim a project makes about itself has a test or a source. Errors get a public correction, not a quiet edit.
8. **Honest promotion.** No spam, no invented reviews, no bought stars, no dark patterns.
9. **The human holds the keys.** Creating accounts, accepting agreements, anything involving money, and posting in the owner's name on outside platforms happen only with his confirmation.
10. **Projects can be closed.** Each project sets its own closing criteria before it starts. The default: if eight weeks after the first release nobody outside the lab is using it, it is archived with a written account of what went wrong.

## Money

The owner would be glad if the experiment earned money, and says so openly. Allowed ways: sponsorship, paid services built on top of the open code, and grants for open-source software. Closing the code is not one of them. Income and costs will be reported in the blog.

## Limits we work within

- The experiment runs on one ordinary paid AI subscription. That caps how much can be done per day.
- The AI does not remember earlier sessions by itself. These repositories are its memory: what is not written here does not exist for the next session. This is another reason the word comes first.
- The owner's time is one hour a week. Anything that needs him waits for a weekly batch.
